## The problem

Motorized wheelchairs solve mobility, not reach. Everyday tasks — grabbing something off a shelf, picking an object up off the floor — stay out of reach for a lot of wheelchair users, and most assistive arms on the market are built for one specific chair rather than working across chairs people already own.

## The build

A robotic arm designed to mount to any motorized wheelchair, built around a ball-screw and linear-rail system for precise, stable reach, driven by CAN-networked quasi-direct-drive (QDD) actuators with servos handling the finer joints. The end effector uses 3D-printed TPU grippers, soft enough to handle a range of everyday objects without a fragile grip.

## The approach

Universality across wheelchairs was the design constraint from the start, which is why the mount and drive system are being built independent of any one chair's geometry. QDD actuators over CAN were chosen over standard hobby servos for the primary joints because they give backdrivable, torque-controlled motion — important for something operating right next to a person. AI-assisted ease-of-use features (object detection, assisted grasping) are planned once the mechanical platform is validated.
