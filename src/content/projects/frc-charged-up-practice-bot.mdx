## The problem

Building a full FRC robot solo means owning every subsystem (drivetrain, scoring mechanism, pneumatics, wiring) with no division of labor to fall back on if one piece runs into trouble.

## The build

A complete, competition-ready robot for FRC's "Charged Up" game: a seesaw elevator arm, a pneumatic claw for game-piece manipulation, and a full drivebase, with all wiring and pneumatics accounted for end to end.

## The approach

The arm pivots as a single long boom from a fixed point near the chassis, seesawing up and down to set its angle, while a separate cable-and-pulley carriage runs along its length to extend reach without needing a second full-size arm stage. That pulley and drive sprocket sit right at the pivot, so the motor driving the extension doesn't add weight out at the tip where it would fight the arm's own leverage. The claw at the far end is a simple two-finger pincer, actuated pneumatically rather than with a servo or motor, which gives it a fast, consistent open/close action under game-time pressure without needing precise position control. The drivebase runs four wheels at the corners of a plated chassis for stable support under the arm's extended weight.
