## The problem

Standard prosthetic feet are passive. They don't actively correct for uneven terrain or shifting balance the way a natural foot and ankle do, which leaves users compensating with the rest of their body.

## The build

StablStep actively balances across multiple axes, something standard prosthetics can't do. A PID control loop handles real-time stabilization, while a machine learning layer predicts the wearer's steps and adjusts the foot's position in advance rather than only reacting after the fact. The mechanism combines 3D-printed structure with aluminum for the load-bearing components, driven by an Arduino-based control system.

## The approach

The ankle joint is spring-damped rather than rigid: a shock-absorber-style cartridge sits between the foot paddle and the upper ankle block, so the mechanism has some passive give built in before the PID loop even has to react, similar to how a real ankle has passive compliance from tendons and cartilage before muscles engage. The printed prototype shown here tests that mechanism directly on an Arduino Uno with a breadboard before it's folded into the full servo and sensor stack from the CAD model, which is the right order to build it in: prove the passive mechanics work and feel right under load first, then layer active correction on top rather than debugging both at once.
