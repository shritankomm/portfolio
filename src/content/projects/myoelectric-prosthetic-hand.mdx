## The problem

Prosthetic hands need to translate a wearer's own muscle activity into individual finger motion, a first step into medical robotics that touches sensing, mechanism design, and control all at once.

## The build

A prosthetic hand sized to my own hand, reading muscle signals through an EMG sensor and driving each finger independently via six MG996R servos through a tendon-driven mechanism, controlled by an Arduino.

## The approach

Each finger is built as a chain of pin-hinged segments, so a single tendon can flex the whole finger by pulling through every joint at once rather than needing a separate actuator per knuckle. That's what makes six servos enough to drive a full hand: one servo and one tendon per finger, plus a separate servo and joint for the thumb, which sits rotated on the side of the palm rather than in line with the fingers so it can oppose them the way a real thumb does. The EMG sensor reads raw muscle activity as an analog signal, which the Arduino thresholds and maps to a simple open or close command per channel rather than trying to reconstruct fine proportional control from a noisy signal on a first pass. The palm itself is a flat structural plate with mounting feet at the wrist end, keeping the hand's own geometry simple so the finger mechanisms are what carry the design.
